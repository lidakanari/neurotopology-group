Fractal growth controls

How to play · full rules

Fractal growth: every active tip advances automatically. After its generation-specific interval it splits into two daughters at symmetric angles. Each daughter inherits a shorter interval, creating recursive self-similarity.

Adjust the angle, interval, length ratio, noise, upward bias and death probability before or during growth. A branch stops if it would intersect another branch or hit a side or bottom boundary. The board scrolls upward. No collision ends the entire run unless all tips have stopped.

Capture gold resources (+1) and rare blue resources (+5) with the same capture radii as Neuron Brewer. Twenty resources remain available ahead of the growth front. Growth uses a shared 1,000-unit budget; reaching it awards the same completion bonus. Results include resource statistics, optimal wiring comparison and TMD barcodes.

    Why do neurons grow like trees?

    Created by Dr. Lida Kanari
    University of Oxford

    Many natural systems grow as branching trees. Roots and branches spread through space to reach sunlight, water, or nutrients, while their shape reflects a trade-off between reaching new territory and the cost of building more structure.

    Neurons also form intricate trees. Their dendrites receive signals from other cells, while their axons carry signals outward. To connect with many partners, neurons extend and branch, yet growth requires material, energy, and space. Their final shapes emerge from growth rules, constraints, and interactions with their surroundings.

    In this simplified game, the glowing points stand for opportunities to capture resources. Golden points are worth 1; the rarer blue points are worth 5. Every growing tip consumes a limited budget: branching reaches more locations, but uses that budget faster. Your challenge is to balance exploration, efficiency, and collision avoidance. This is an interactive analogy, not a literal biological simulation.
